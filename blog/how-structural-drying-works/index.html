How Structural Drying Works in Minneapolis, MN

Home / Blog / Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Minneapolis, MN

Ask people around Minneapolis, MN what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. Here we cover how professional drying works and the reasons it outperforms a do-it-yourself effort by a wide margin.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Psychrometry, a science concerned with gas-vapor mixtures and their thermodynamic and physical properties, is what structural drying is built on. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Consumer dehumidifiers and fans do not compare to the machines a restoration crew brings. An hour of run time takes 15 to 30 or more pints out of the air with one LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifier, against 2 to 3 pints with a household model. To make wet surfaces evaporate faster, high-velocity air movers push 2,500 CFM or more of air exactly where it is aimed. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. All of it costs tens of thousands, and using it well takes specific training.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. The opening step is a full assessment: thermal imaging and meters locate and map each wet area. Technicians use the map to place equipment and to record starting moisture levels as a baseline. Next, psychrometric calculations decide the position of each air mover and dehumidifier so that water evaporates and is removed efficiently. Sometimes the space is heated so evaporation goes faster. Each day a technician comes back, reads every monitoring point, moves or changes equipment if required, and writes down the progress. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

How Long Does Drying Take?

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days with proper commercial equipment. Several things set the schedule: the amount and kind of equipment in use, the way the building was built, surrounding conditions, the materials involved, with concrete drying much more slowly than drywall, and the quantity of water present. Typical figures are 5 to 7 days or more for concrete, 3 to 4 days for wood framing, and 2 to 3 days for standard drywall. You will get a projected finish date from the initial readings, and it is revised as the structure dries.

Why Monitoring Matters

The difference between a professional job and guessing is monitoring every day. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. A wall or floor may seem dry under your hand and still be dangerously wet deeper inside. Only calibrated moisture meters can provide this verification. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
