Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Minneapolis

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Minneapolis team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
How We Fix It

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.
