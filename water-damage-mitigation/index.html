The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. Our Minneapolis crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Find the main water valve and make sure it has not seized.
  • A monthly look around toilets and inside sink cabinets catches drips, staining and spongy flooring.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Signs You Need Water Damage Mitigation in Minneapolis

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Set Up Containment

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.
