Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Minneapolis, Minneapolis Restoration Co is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Minneapolis

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
