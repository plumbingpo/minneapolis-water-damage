Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Minneapolis

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. Our team in Minneapolis finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Inside the Wall

Mold thrives in a wall cavity that is both damp and dark. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Damaged Flooring

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Open What Is Needed

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the framing and subfloor reach normal moisture readings, usually 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Treat and Document

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and you receive photos along with the moisture logs. That paperwork proves the area was dry ahead of repairs and can support a claim.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.
